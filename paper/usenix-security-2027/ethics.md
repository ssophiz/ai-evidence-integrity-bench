# Ethics appendix draft

This study is intended to measure and reduce trust failures using synthetic evidence and inert downstream decisions. It has not yet completed data, participant, or release review. Record the determinations before collection and revise this appendix to describe what actually occurred.

## People and data

Use synthetic Korean-English material with no real investigations, personal data, credentials, malware, or operational targets. Verify provenance and redistribution rights for any later public-text additions. Public availability alone does not remove privacy obligations. Synthetic names and scenarios must not imply misconduct by identifiable people or organizations.

The stakeholders include people whose evidence may later be summarized, examiners who rely on findings, annotators, model providers, and researchers reproducing the work. Lost uncertainty can cause an unsupported allegation; excessive fail-closed rejection can hide useful evidence. Report promotion, omissions, availability, and utility together so neither harm disappears behind a single security score.

Recruit two Korean-English adjudicators and a bilingual resolver with relevant language proficiency. Provide task information, appropriate compensation, confidentiality guidance, and a way to stop or decline disturbing material. Keep identity and payment records outside the research artifact. Determine and document whether institutional human-participant review is required; do not invent an approval or exemption. Separate personnel consent from the scientific judgment that a rubric is reliable.

## Controlled evaluation

Use isolated workflows and simulated decisions throughout. Limit adaptive evaluation to the declared synthetic corpus and research systems. Do not contact third parties, execute embedded content, or allow generated findings to change real investigative or administrative decisions. Record provider terms and any applicable model-license constraints before model access.

Preserve unsuccessful attempts, invalid outputs, disagreements, and deviations. Do not discard inconvenient failures or relabel abstention as semantic correctness. Human authors must verify all generated prose, code, references, and numerical claims; responsibility for the submission remains with them. [USENIX AI-use and ethics policy](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity27/call-for-papers).

## Release and use

Review the final artifact for personal information, identifying metadata, and capabilities unnecessary for reproducing the defensive evaluation. Prefer inert local examples and complete outcome records. Explain any justified omission in the Open Science appendix and its effect on reproducibility. If a concrete issue affecting a deployed service is incidentally identified, pause that activity and establish a disclosure plan before further interaction or publication.

The study cannot establish evidentiary admissibility, the truth of source claims, or suitability for operational investigations. A typed label and a source ID do not prove semantic faithfulness. State those limits beside results and in the artifact instructions, and describe negative outcomes with the same care as improvements.
